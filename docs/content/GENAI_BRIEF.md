## Every lesson: the ten parts (checked by `catalog/genai-module.test.ts`)
Run as you go: `CHECK_GENAI_MODULE=<n> npx jest --no-watchman src/features/courses/content/catalog/genai-module` (also runs the standard rules on your lessons).
`intro` (2 to 4 verb-first objectives = part 1 **Learning objectives**, one-to-two-sentence `why`), `summary` (part 9, the lesson summary), `minutes` 12 to 30 (about 15 to 20 hours overall), `skills`.
`body` (about 600 to 1000 words of explanation, not a list of definitions) is, in this order:
1. an opening **paragraph**.
2. heading **A real situation**: part 3, a concrete believable moment of someone using (or misusing) the idea, with the actual prompt or what the AI said, as an *invented* example.
3. heading **How it works**: part 2, the concept for a beginner: why before how, one honest analogy, a method the learner can use tomorrow. No jargon without a definition. Transferable ideas, not one product's buttons.
   Part 4 (**visuals**): at least one `diagram`, `visual` (existing ids: `ai-tokens`, `ai-model-input-output`, `ai-neural-network`, `ai-embeddings`, `ai-rag`, `ai-llm-workflow`, `ai-app-workflow`; new, being built: `ai-context-window`, `ai-prompt-anatomy`, `ai-image-prompt-builder`, `ai-agent-loop`: use them only in the lessons named in "Visuals" below) or `table`.
   Level pieces (`audience`, see the adaptation brief): a gentler beginner walk-through (`{ max: 'beginner' }`) and a deeper "in practice" block for advanced learners.
4. heading **AI in Action**: part 5. One `demo` block `{ type: 'demo', title, prompt, output, notice }` (optionally two) showing a prompt and the *kind* of answer it gets, with `notice` telling the learner what to look at. **A demo is
   a hand-written illustration, never a real model's output**: do not say "ChatGPT replied" or present it as recorded; the app labels it "Illustration". Make the output realistic *including a flaw* where the lesson is about checking.
5. heading **Try it yourself**: part 6, guided practice: a `prompt` block the learner copies into their own assistant (with their own situation in brackets) written so they do the thinking, plus 1 to 2 sentences on what to look for
   and what AI gets wrong here. (The lesson's Prompt Playground exercise, part 7, is a `prompt-lab` activity, below.)
6. exactly one `takeaways` block (2 to 6 items) after "Try it yourself".
7. optional heading **Go further** (part 10) as the LAST body part: a `details`, `list` or paragraph of optional exploration (further reading goes through `version`-style official links only when you checked them; otherwise describe what to try). **Optional coding exercises, only in modules 2, 7 and 10,** may be `code` blocks here (JavaScript or Python, runnable by someone who wants to, labelled optional, no secrets, a placeholder for any key). `code` blocks anywhere else fail the check.
Then, as for every course: `activity` blocks, `quiz`, `task`.
- **Activities** (part 6/7): at least two different kinds per lesson. Existing kinds you may use: `scenario` (exactly one `best`, one or two `acceptable`, tempting `poor` ones with consequences), `match` (situation to concept), `tap-reveal`, `arrange`/`fill-blank` with `language: 'text'`.
  New: **`prompt-lab`** `{ kind: 'prompt-lab', labId, id, title, prompt, hints (2+), explanation, concept }`: a live prompt exercise on a lab you write (below); `hints` coach the approach, never contain a model prompt. **`workflow-builder`**: see below.
  Ids `<lesson-id>-<kind>-<n>`. Each activity has 3 progressive hints, an explanation and a concept. `predict-output`, `code-challenge` etc. are not for this course.
- **Quiz** (part 8, the knowledge check): 3 to 5 scenario questions testing judgment ("Which prompt change fixes this?", "What should you check in this answer?"), 3 or 4 choices, varied correct slot, an explanation. Never trivia, never "all of the above", nothing the lesson did not teach.
- **Task:** a short real-world thing to do (use an assistant for a real need, check an output, change a setting), 2+ steps, 5 to 20 minutes, then reflect.

## Prompt labs (`practice/labs/module-NN.ts`)
Read `LabTask` in `practice/types.ts` closely. A lab is a *live* run: the learner edits `starterPrompt` and the server runs their prompt together with the `material` as data, with your `system` framing. Rules:
- `id` `lab-NN-<kebab>` (NN = your module number, 2 digits; unique; **each lab used by exactly one `prompt-lab` activity**, and the module check reports labs used 0 or 2 times). `experiment` is one of playground, text-generation, summarization, classification, document-analysis, structured-output, tool-use.
- `material` (when the experiment needs it; at most 3,000 characters) is **fictional**: a made-up meeting transcript, policy, product sheet, support ticket, price list. No real person, company or private data, ever. Make facts in it checkable (names, dates, numbers) so a hallucination can be *seen*: the learner can compare the output with the material.
- `starterPrompt`: a weak-but-honest first prompt (the learner is expected to improve it). `system`: one to three sentences framing the model as a helpful assistant that follows the user's request using only the material when there is one; **it must not contain the answer or tell the model what the learner's prompt should say**. Server only.
- `output`: `{ format: 'text' }`, or `{ format: 'json', fields }` for structured-output/classification (fields checked by code: required, type, `oneOf`). `key` (server only) holds the correct values for classification and structured output (`expected` per field name; strings or string arrays), compared by code after the run and shown as "what the material says" after the review: write only keys the material truly supports.
- `facts` (server only, summarization and document-analysis): 3 to 8 facts the material states, so the review can separate supported claims from invented ones. `tools` (tool-use only, at most 3): `{ name, description, params, fixtures }`; the server answers a call only from `fixtures` (a call that matches none gets "not found"), so make at least one realistic question that the fixtures **cannot** answer.
- `compare`: `'prompts'` (two prompts A and B on the same material) where the lesson is about what a change in the prompt does; `'models'` (the same prompt on two model tiers, measured live: latency and token counts) only where the module asks for it. Never describe results of a comparison before it is run; the screen shows the learner's own run.
- `criteria` (3 to 6, `{ id, label }`): what a strong prompt does here, the checklist the learner sees and the review scores ("States the audience", "Asks for a table with named columns"). `tips` (3 to 5, public): suggestions for improving the prompt, shown after the first run, written as the teacher you are, not as model output. `example`: `{ prompt, outcome }` a hand-written strong prompt and a description of what its answer looks like (labelled Illustration in the app; do not write it as a verbatim real output). `verify` (2 to 4): things to check in ANY output of this task.
- `maxRuns` 2 to 8 (compare labs 4 to 8). `difficulty` mixes gentle/realistic/demanding. At least one `gentle` per module with 3+ labs.
- Never ask for anything unsafe (no real credentials, no medical, legal or financial advice generation as a deliverable, no personal data of real people). A lab never needs an image, audio or a file: where the lesson is about those, the `material` is the text a tool *extracted* from them (a transcript, the text read from a photographed receipt, a PDF's extracted table) and the activity says so.

## Workflow builders (`kind: 'workflow-builder'`, in the lesson body)
Palette of 8 to 12 parts across the lanes input, ai, tool, human, output, with **tempting wrong parts** (an AI step that sends the message without review, a step that pastes a password into the prompt, "trust the AI's number", no log). `rules`: `require` (parts that must be in), `forbid` (unsafe parts), `before` (`[a, b]`: a before b, e.g. human review before send): each with a `message` that explains *why* in one sentence. `solution` is one valid order and must satisfy all rules (validated); `minSteps` 3+, `maxSteps` within the palette; `simulation` is a hand-written sample request, one line per solution step, and the final output (the app labels it "Simulated run, not a real model"). Every workflow ends with a human decision wherever the outcome affects a person, money or reputation.

## Modules: required AI work (more is welcome, less is not)
(L prompt lab · W workflow builder · V visual.) Lab counts are minimums for the module.
1. **Introduction** (5): ≥2 L (L1.3 a `compare: 'models'` lab on one everyday task, with a lesson on choosing by use case and *measuring on your own task*; L1.4 a `playground` lab: a vague prompt vs a specific one, with `compare: 'prompts'`). Lesson 3 uses a `toolComparison` block (tools `chatgpt`, `claude`, `gemini`, ids in `content/ai-dev/tools.ts`) plus `version` notes (dated 2026-11-17, official sources, no prices) for any product fact; Hugging Face, Midjourney and Runway are described by use case without feature claims, or with a sourced `version` note.
2. **How it works** (6): ≥2 L (a `text-generation` lab where the learner runs the same prompt twice and sees the answers differ; a `document-analysis` lab where the question is NOT answerable from the material, to meet hallucination: the key idea is "does it say it doesn't know?"). V: `ai-neural-network` (2.1), `ai-model-input-output` (2.3), `ai-tokens` (2.4), `ai-context-window` (2.5). Optional "Go further" code allowed.
3. **Prompt engineering** (7): ≥5 L: 3.1 playground `compare: 'prompts'`, 3.2 playground (role, context, task, constraints; V `ai-prompt-anatomy`), 3.3 `classification` few-shot (json), 3.4 `structured-output` (json fields checked), 3.5 `text-generation` chain (two sentences of method; the learner runs step by step), 3.6 refine (`compare: 'prompts'`), 3.7 evaluation: a `classification` lab whose `material` holds several numbered test cases and whose `key` is the right label for each, so the learner *measures* their prompt.
4. **Image generation** (5): images are not generated in the app. ≥1 L (a text lab: the learner writes a prompt that makes the model turn a client brief into an image prompt; criteria are subject, style, composition, light, what to avoid), V `ai-image-prompt-builder` (4.2), `scenario`/`match`/`arrange` activities on prompts and editing choices, `demo` blocks as illustrations (describe what an image would show in words). Teach the concepts (diffusion described as gradually removing noise, guided by the text) as an *analogy*, honestly, and say that practice with a real image tool happens outside Talino, using the "Try it yourself" prompts as the exercise. Tool names (Midjourney, Runway, Firefly...) only as examples of use cases; no feature or price claims unless in a sourced `version` note.
5. **Video and audio** (5): ≥2 L (5.1 a `text-generation` lab: write a narration script ready for text-to-speech with pronunciation notes; 5.2 a `structured-output` lab: turn a messy *transcript* (given as material) into action items; 5.5 a script/shot-list lab). Same honesty as module 4. **Project P1** (below).
6. **Multimodal** (5): ≥2 L, each using the *text extracted* from an image, document or recording as `material` and saying so (6.2 a receipt's extracted text, 6.3 a PDF's extracted table, 6.4 a call transcript). Teach checking: OCR/transcription errors become the model's errors.
7. **RAG** (6): ≥2 L (`document-analysis`: answer only from the supplied passages, cite which, and say "not in the documents"; a grounded-answer lab with one unanswerable question), 1 W (the RAG pipeline: ingest, chunk, embed, store, retrieve, answer with citations, with wrong parts such as "let the model answer from memory when nothing is found"). V: `ai-embeddings` (7.2), `ai-rag` (7.4). Optional "Go further" code allowed. **Project P2** (below).
8. **Agents** (6): ≥2 L `tool-use` (one with a tool that can answer, one where the tool cannot), ≥2 W (a customer-support agent; an approval gate before an irreversible action), V `ai-agent-loop` (8.3).
9. **Business** (5): ≥2 L (a `structured-output` ticket triage, a `text-generation` brand-voice email with `compare: 'prompts'`), ≥2 W (support triage; a content approval workflow). **Project P3** (below).
10. **Building apps** (6): ≥2 L (`structured-output` with fields checked, a `tool-use` call), ≥1 W (the architecture of an AI feature: the phone/browser calls *your* server, the server holds the key and calls the model, the app validates the reply; wrong parts: key inside the app, no limit, trusting the reply). V: `ai-app-workflow` (10.1). Optional "Go further" code allowed (JavaScript `fetch` with `process.env`/placeholder keys, never a real key). **Project P4**.
11. **Responsible AI** (6): ≥3 L (a `document-analysis` verification lab where the learner must make the model separate supported from unsupported claims; a bias lab `compare: 'prompts'` on a hiring-style sentence rewritten neutrally; a privacy `structured-output` lab that lists personal details found in a fictional message, `key` = the list), ≥1 W (a safe workflow that redacts and has a human review before anything is sent to a model). Practical, not preachy: no legal advice, say where rules differ by country and where to read the official text; copyright taught as principles and questions to ask, not rulings.
12. **Real world and future** (5): ≥1 L (12.3 a `compare: 'models'` lab with the evaluation method: criteria first, then run, then judge), ≥1 W (12.2 or 12.5), the capstone lesson prepares P4's roadmap. Case studies are **illustrative, invented composites** labelled as such; no real company claims, no statistics, no predictions presented as fact: "what could change" is written as questions to keep asking.

## Accuracy and honesty (these are checked by a person, not a test)
- **No benchmark numbers, rankings, "best model" claims, prices, release dates or percentages.** Capabilities differ and change: teach the *dimensions* (response quality, instruction following, context handling, multimodal ability, cost and latency, fit for the use) and how to test on the learner's own task. If a product fact is essential, it goes in a `version` block (date 2026-11-17, official URL, no price) and you say plainly that it can change; otherwise leave it out. Never state what any product "can't" do from memory.
- Teach how models work correctly and modestly: a language model predicts likely next tokens from patterns learned in training; it does not look facts up unless connected to a tool; fluent does not mean true. Analogies are labelled as analogies. Diffusion, embeddings and attention are explained by what they do, not with maths. Training data, bias, copyright and safety are described as open problems where they are.
- No invented statistics, studies, quotes or company claims. Invented examples are labelled as invented where they could be mistaken for fact. No real or famous person's name in an example.
- Never present an AI output as proof of the learner's skill. Say that results in the app's labs are the learner's prompt and a real model's reply *as of that run*, that another run may differ, and that the review is a guide, not a grade.
- Tali and Tala: never write their dialogue. Use "your AI assistant", "an AI assistant" for the learner's own tool.
- **English only.** Write no Filipino or Tagalog (owner's standing rule).
- Report at the end: lessons written, activities by kind, labs and workflows by experiment, anything you were unsure about, the check output. Do not claim to have run the engine; you can't.
